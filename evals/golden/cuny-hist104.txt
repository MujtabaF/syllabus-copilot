Introduction:
   The goal of this course is to provide students with a broad understanding of American
History from 1865 until the present day. Focusing both on the domestic front and the nation’s
changing place in the world affairs, the class will return to a number of themes: America’s
growing economic and military power in the world (as well as the limits of this power); the
development of a mass, national culture; the struggle to win the rights of citizenship for all
Americans, regardless of race, class, creed or sex; and the efforts of America’s politicians and the
national government to manage the changing political, economic, cultural and international
situation. We will learn about well-known American figures as well as everyday citizens who
shaped the country’s history.
   This course meets the “Analyzing Social Structures” and the “United States Context of
Experience” requirements for PLAS. It also fulfills the CUNY-Pathways Flexible Core
Requirement for “U.S. Experience in its Diversity.”

Course Requirements:
 A.   Students will practice one of the most fundamental skills of the historian: learn to analyze
   primary sources by correctly identifying the type of source, from when and where it
   originated, its intended audience, the probable motives of the author, and author’s
   assumptions or biases.
B.   Students will examine the position of history as a discipline in the liberal arts curriculum
   and in society, in the context of the development of history as a profession in United
   States in the modern era.
C.   Students will generalize material from the history of modern U.S. to consider broader
   questions such as how states and societies change and develop over time, the roles played
   by governmental and societal organizations, and the role of ideas and social and
   economic structures within human societies.
D.   Students will compare developments between different countries and regions in the US
   and contextualize American developments within global events. Course materials and
   assignments will focus on identifying and examining the diversity of experience in

graded essays, and short in-class activities and exercises.
